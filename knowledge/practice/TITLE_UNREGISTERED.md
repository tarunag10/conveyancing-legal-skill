# Unregistered land, epitome, root and historic charges

**Load when:** no registered title, first registration, auction unregistered property, historical mortgages, missing deeds or ambiguous endorsed releases. Authority: Land Charges Act 1972 and HMLR PG1, PG2, PG63, PG31, plus verified transaction-specific deeds.

## First task: prove it is unregistered

Carry out index map search (SIM) over correctly identified land, not merely a postal-address search. Assess adjacent/overlapping registered titles and any pending first registration. Check whether a compulsory first-registration trigger occurred historically. Do not assume a document bundle labelled "unregistered" proves that description.

## Epitome review method

1. Assemble deeds in chronological order with full name of each party, dates, parcels, consideration, interest conveyed, burdens and benefit, attached plans, execution and schedules. Tabulate **instrument, parties, date, land, effect, page and missing evidence**.
2. Identify a qualifying good root of title for the chain applicable to the matter. Do not assume any instrument over 15 years is automatically adequate: assess ability to deal with the whole legal estate, accurate land description and adequacy under current law/practice.
3. For every later transaction, check continuity of ownership and changes of name, deaths, corporate successors, powers of attorney, assents, trust appointments and legal estate. Compare each plan with the proposed transfer extent.
4. Assess legal charges, equitable mortgages, further advances and all-monies provisions. Search each relevant estate owner and relevant name variants/periods. HMLR PG63 explains K15 searches and bankruptcy entries. A negative search certificate has legal effect within its actual names, areas and dates, not outside them.
5. Where deeds are missing or destroyed, consider HMLR PG2, alternative probative evidence, truth statements and likely class of title. Escalate first registration complexity.

## Historic charge decision table

- **Document shows only creation of a mortgage or further charge:** treat as subsisting unless sufficient release evidence, relevant statutory consequences and searches prove otherwise.
- **Correspondence says charge 'vacated':** assertion only. Demand the endorsed charge with complete words of receipt/release, discharge deed or other acceptable evidence; examine signatures, dates, chargee identity, assignment and registration implications.
- **There is an endorsement:** transcribe its exact words, page and maker, assess whether it releases full charge/part, and verify that its formalities are acceptable under the rules relevant to that charge. Do not assume the endorsement is dispositive merely because it appears on a scanned instrument.
- **Historic second/further charge:** search/release separately. A first charge release does not necessarily dispose of a further charge or different lender.
- **Mortgage merged or lender renamed:** trace corporate identity/assignment; do not substitute a similarity of names for evidence of capacity.

## Land Charges search design

Compile each owner from root to current seller, all intervening legal proprietors, aliases, former names, county names and relevant date ranges. Confirm K15 scope, original effective protection, entries returned and whether any statutory exception applies. Bankruptcy-only K16 has a different purpose. Use official search certificates and not merely a portal screen summary.

## FR1 registration package

The application normally involves FR1, form DL **in duplicate**, qualifying title deed bundle including original supporting documentation under relevant HMLR practice, plans sufficiently identifying land, accurate fee and applications for ancillary registrable interests as necessary. HMLR PG1 §4.4 controls supporting evidence; individual circumstances can differ. Ensure certified copies where needed and original documents where required. Track HMLR requisitions and confirm class of title granted on completion.

## Report-on-title language

Separate (i) present proved rights and title; (ii) documents missing/unclear; (iii) defects requiring a seller remedy before completion; (iv) evidence/lender approval needed; and (v) exact registrations to perfect security. A buyer's contractual deadline does not turn documentary uncertainty into an acceptable title.
