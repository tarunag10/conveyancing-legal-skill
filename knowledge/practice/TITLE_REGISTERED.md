# Registered title investigation and lender-ready reports

**Load when:** purchaser/borrower title review, report on title, conflicting plans, easements, restrictions, charge release, restrictive covenants or mortgage lender title questions. Legal sources: Land Registration Act 2002 s27; HMLR PG19, PG31, PG12, PG8; actual filed instruments and official copies.

## Source inventory

Obtain dated official copy register and plan for **each** relevant freehold and leasehold title; filed deeds referred to by entries; original transfers/leases where necessary; agreed contract plan; survey/inspection and searches; sale particulars only as a cross-check. Capture each document's title number, date, date of register edition, date of search and sender. A title plan usually depicts **general** boundaries, not definitive surveyed lines.

## Investigate and report by register

### Property Register

- Identify property description, freehold/leasehold tenure, easements expressly granted/benefiting land, land tinted/coloured and filed transfers. Compare postal address with legal extent and transfer property description. Related freehold title is not automatically the title being acquired.
- Identify missing express rights for access, drainage, services, support and entry to repair. Trace rights through deeds, registered entries, plans, grants, implied or prescriptive claims **without assuming** implied rights apply.
- On leasehold titles verify date, parties, term and commencement, superior title and variations. Explain difference between lease granted date, term commencement and unexpired term.

### Proprietorship Register

- Verify class of title, registered owner exactly, capacity, transfer price entry only as historic context, and every restriction. For each restriction record *exact operative wording*, whose certificate/consent is required, who can issue it and evidence of satisfaction. A Form A restriction prompts beneficial ownership/overreaching review, not a blanket objection to the sale.
- Identify overseas entity/company status and possible statutory restrictions. Verify actual registration and relevant exceptions; do not guess from a company name.

### Charges Register

- Extract charges and further charges separately (each party, registration/deed date, beneficiary, all-monies wording, priority, land affected). Distinguish a restrictive covenant or notice from a financial charge.
- Obtain actual release/discharge evidence: HM Land Registry cancellation, satisfactory e-DS1/DS1, DS3 for release of part or other acceptable evidence applying HMLR PG31. An email that a charge has been "vacated" does not alone clear the matter. Note lender redemption mechanics and whether seller solicitor undertakes to discharge.
- For covenants record date, beneficiary/dominant land if known, precise burden, restrictions on proposed use or development, whether title indemnity or consent is feasible. Do **not** approach a beneficiary or council if doing so may prejudice available indemnity cover without instructions.

## Access and highways test

Test three propositions **separately**: (A) physical frontage or access onto a road, (B) the legal right to pass over any intervening land, and (C) whether the road is highway maintainable at public expense or whether a private estate-road arrangement is acceptable. Obtain a highway extent/adoption response or other reliable highway authority evidence as appropriate, and trace the access route on the title plan and any express rights. A street-coloured plan is not conclusive of adoption, and abutment on a plan is not proof of available lawful vehicular access. Report exceptions to lender using lender's own criteria.

## Other legal and practical tests

- Boundaries: access gaps, encroachments, overhanging structures, garage parking and any conflict with measured plan; apply HMLR PG40 or appropriate plan guidance if necessary.
- Overriding interests and occupiers: ask who occupies, terms and consents; ensure vacant possession/tenancy align with contract and lender valuation.
- Easements: ability to use and maintain services/roads; express reservations for neighbours; development ransom strip concerns.
- Rentcharges, estate management charges and potential statutory remedies; confirm the exact instrument and registration protections.
- Planning permissions, lawful use, building regulation completion certificates, FENSA/equivalents, warranty, conservation/listed status, highways and environmental checks are evidence-dependent.
- Indemnity cover: identify risk, wording, insured parties, successors, lender inclusion, exclusions (notably prior disclosure/contact), and whether insurance is acceptable instead of cure.

## Output format

| Entry/document locator | Fact strictly established | Unverified issue | Consequence | Enquiry/remedy | Risk | Lender consent/report? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Then include 1. purchase/borrower security identity; 2. linked titles; 3. proprietary title; 4. financial burdens; 5. rights/access; 6. adverse interests and restrictions; 7. searches; 8. leasehold features; 9. proposed remedy and evidence; 10. readiness status and specific outstanding lender requirements.

## Draft examples (do not assert as facts)

**Discharge question:** "Please provide the executed release or other registration-acceptable evidence discharging the charge dated [date] in favour of [lender], together with confirmation of the basis on which all further-charge obligations secured against the property will be released on completion. Your statement that the charge was vacated does not presently establish its discharge."

**Access question:** "Please identify the complete legal and physical access route from the property to the public highway, provide the plan/deed granting any necessary rights over intervening land, and supply confirmation of highway status or adoption to the extent required by the lender."
